# G7 Science (Chemistry Unit) Exam Syllabus

> [!ABSTRACT] Overview
> Based on the provided course materials, this syllabus covers core concepts from the **composition of matter** to the **periodic table trends** and **chemical reactions**.

## I. Classification of Matter
*(Elements, Compounds and Mixtures)*

> [!NOTE] Basic Definitions
> - **Element**: Consists of only **one type of atom**. It can be independent atoms or molecules made of identical atoms (e.g., $O_2, N_2$).
> - **Compound**: Formed by atoms of **different elements chemically bonded** together (e.g., $H_2O, CO_2$).
> - **Mixture**: A combination of two or more substances that are **not chemically bonded** and are usually easy to separate (e.g., air, salt water).

*   **Identification & Diagrams**: Ability to distinguish between pure substances (elements or compounds) and mixtures based on particle model diagrams.

## II. Atomic Structure and History

### History of Atomic Discovery
Understand the evolution of the atomic model over 2400 years:
1.  **Democritus**: Atomism
2.  **Dalton**: Atomic Theory
3.  **Thomson**: Discovery of Electron
4.  **Rutherford**: Discovery of Nucleus
5.  **Bohr**: Electron Shells
6.  **Schrödinger and Heisenberg**: Modern Quantum Models

### Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Charge | Mass |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proton** | Positive (+) | ~1 |
| **Neutron** | Neutral (0) | ~1 |
| **Electron** | Negative (-) | ~0 (negligible) |

> [!IMPORTANT] Neutrality Principle
> Atoms are electrically neutral overall because **Number of Protons = Number of Electrons**.

### Atomic Number & Mass Number
*   **Atomic Number** = Number of Protons.
*   **Mass Number** = Number of Protons + Number of Neutrons.
*   **Isotopes**: Atoms of the same element with the **same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons** (e.g., Carbon-12, Carbon-13).

## III. Electron Configuration & Periodic Table Trends

### Electron Configuration
*   Master the electron shell arrangement for the first 20 elements.
    *   **Max Capacity**: 2 electrons in the 1st shell, usually 8 in subsequent shells.
*   **Rules**:
    *   **Group number** = Number of outer shell electrons.
    *   **Period number** = Number of electron shells.

### Organization of the Periodic Table
*   **Mendeleev's Contribution (1869)**: **Left gaps** to predict undiscovered elements (e.g., Gallium).
*   **Regions**:
    *   **Metals**: Left side
    *   **Non-metals**: Right side
    *   **Metalloids**: Staircase line between metals and non-metals.

---

## IV. Group Trends

### Group 1: Alkali Metals
*   **Members**: Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), Potassium (K), etc.
*   **Physical Trends** (Moving Down $\downarrow$):
    *   Softness $\uparrow$ (Increases)
    *   Density $\uparrow$ (Increases)
    *   Melting Point $\downarrow$ (Decreases)
*   **Chemical Reactions**:
    *   React with water to produce **hydrogen gas** and **alkaline hydroxides**.
    *   Example: $2Li + 2H_2O \rightarrow 2LiOH + H_2$
    *   **Reactivity**: Increases as atomic number increases.

### Group 7: Halogens
*   **Members**: Fluorine (F), Chlorine (Cl), Bromine (Br), Iodine (I), etc.
*   **Physical Properties**:
    *   Exist as **diatomic molecules** (e.g., $Cl_2$).
    *   **State Changes**: Gas $\rightarrow$ Liquid $\rightarrow$ Solid (moving down).
    *   **Color**: Darkens moving down.
*   **Displacement Reactions**:
    *   A more reactive halogen can displace a less reactive halogen from a compound.
    *   *Example*: Chlorine will displace Bromine.

### Group 0: Noble Gases
*   **Properties**: Stable electron structures, **extremely unreactive (Inert)**.
*   **Uses**: Neon lights, Balloons (Helium).

### Transition Metals
*   **Properties**: Good conductors, high density, usually magnetic.
*   **Uses**:
    *   Iron: Construction
    *   Copper: Electrical cables
    *   **Catalysts**: Chromium, Titanium, Nickel.

## V. Formulas, Naming & Equations

### Naming Rules
1.  **Metal first**: The metal element is named first.
2.  **Suffixes**:
    *   **"-ide"**: Compounds of two elements.
    *   **"-ate"**: Compounds of three elements (one being Oxygen).
3.  **Ions**:
    *   **Cations (+)**: Loss of electrons.
    *   **Anions (-)**: Gain of electrons.

### Balancing Equations
> [!WARNING] Core Principle
> The **total number of atoms** must be the same before and after the reaction.
> **Never change subscripts**; only adjust the **coefficients** in front of the chemical formulas.

*   **State Symbols**:
    *   `(s)` Solid
    *   `(l)` Liquid
    *   `(g)` Gas
    *   `(aq)` Aqueous solution
