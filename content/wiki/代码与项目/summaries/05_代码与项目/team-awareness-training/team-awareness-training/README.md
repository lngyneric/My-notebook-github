# Team Awareness Training
## Overview
This is an 8-week meditation and mindfulness program for work teams, published under the [Creative Commons License](LICENSE). Its core goal is to improve participants' focus and stress management skills, with a curriculum rooted in scientific research and participant experience.

## Course Setup
The program runs for 8 weeks, with 8 participants meeting once per week for a 60-minute session led by a teacher who instructs on new practices and facilitates group reflection and learning. Between sessions, participants complete independent homework, primarily consisting of meditation practice.

All content taught in the course is verified via clinical trials or participant lived experience, with a scientific viewpoint as the foundational framing. Teachers measure core KPIs (stress and focus levels) for all participants before and after the course to track outcomes.

### Target Outcomes
The program targets a 25-30% reduction in participant stress and a 30% or greater increase in focus, benchmarks that have been consistently reproduced in past course runs. Results are directly tied to the level of effort each participant invests, as the practices are practical rather than theoretical — consistent active practice is required for improvement, analogous to physical exercise.

## Teacher Eligibility Requirements
To lead this course, instructors must meet the following prerequisites:
- Sufficient time to deliver the program: each 8-week course requires 32 hours of total teacher time.
- ~8 hours per week to stay current on research related to stress, productivity, leadership, and organizational theory.
- An ongoing personal meditation practice.
- A passion for teaching.

No formal certificates or official permission are required to teach this program, though relevant education is helpful. Instructors are advised to be wary of for-profit brands commercializing mindfulness and meditation practices.

## Standard Session Structure
Each 60-minute weekly session follows this flow:
1. **Participant Check-In**: Participants take turns sharing their main current obstacle to staying calm and focused. Other participants practice active listening and ask questions, avoiding premature solutioning to prioritize identifying the root cause of each participant's challenge. Each participant leaves with targeted homework aligned to their specific root cause.
2. **Instruction**: The teacher delivers the week's relevant theory and practice methods.
3. **Group Meditation**: The full group meditates together.
4. **Closing Reflection**: Participants take turns sharing reflections on the week's session.

## Teacher Responsibilities
### Pre-Course
- Announce the program to the team, framing it as support for building calm and focus.
- Recruit 8 participants for the course cohort.
- Book a meeting room for all 8 weekly sessions.
- Send calendar meeting invites to participants.
- Distribute the [pre-course evaluation form](forms.md) along with program information.

### Per Session
- Facilitate all group exercises.
- Instruct on new methods and tools.
- Practice active listening and ask guided questions during group discussions.
- Stay after the session to answer 1-on-1 or additional questions participants did not have time to raise during the group session.
- Document key takeaways from the session and distribute them via email to the full cohort.
- Meditate for at least 20 minutes before leading each session, as instructor calmness has a positive impact on participant state.

### Post-Course
- Distribute the [anonymous post-course feedback form](forms.md) to participants.
- Compile feedback, pre- and post-course KPI measurements, and share the full results as a PDF with the cohort.

## Anticipated Asked Questions (AAQ)
### Can anyone run this course?
Yes, as long as they have the required time and motivation to deliver the program.

### Even if they aren't a Super Certificed Aware Mindfullness (SCAM™) coach?
Yes. No formal certification is required to teach this program.

### But my swami says this should be kept to just a few selected people ...
The program's creator argues that basic mindfulness and meditation practices are accessible to everyone, analogous to jogging or basic nutritional guidance. Most people only need simple, foundational practices (e.g., observing the mind and breath) rather than advanced techniques, and there is no need to keep these methods secret or restricted. Program effectiveness comes primarily from group accountability, teacher motivation, and measurable outcome feedback loops, rather than exclusive or mystical teachings.

### So it doesn't matter who teaches this course?
Teacher experience does impact outcomes: more experienced instructors will typically deliver better results. However, there is no need to frame mindfulness methods as mystical or restricted, and no requirement to pay for official certification to begin teaching meditation.
