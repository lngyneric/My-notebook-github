# Session #3 - Meditation (Team Awareness Training)
## Abstract
This is the third session of the Team Awareness Training curriculum, focused on building mindfulness skills. It addresses the vagueness of the popularized term "mindfulness", provides a practical working definition aligned with the course's goals, covers both formal and informal practice methods, includes structured reflection on prior meditation homework, and assigns new weekly practice tasks.

## Session Overview
The session follows a 60-minute structured agenda, with the following core components:
1. Sharing (10 min): Reflection on the previous week's meditation homework
2. Teaching (20 min): Core concepts of mindfulness
3. Method (20 min): Actionable ways to practice and build mindfulness
4. Reflection (10 min): Group discussion on mindfulness applications for stress management and focus

### Facilitator Requirements
- Document all key insights and findings raised during the session
- Record any unanswered questions to follow up with participants via post-session email

## Session Content Breakdown
### 1. Sharing (Homework Reflection)
- **Facilitation Guide**: Participants take turns clockwise around the table to share their meditation experience from the prior week, including difficulty level, challenges finding time to practice, and any observed changes in stressful situations.
- **Ground Rules**: Allow each participant to speak fully without rushing; avoid steering their reflections. Do not shame participants for incomplete homework, instead coach them to identify feasible practice windows (e.g., at home, at work, during commutes or breaks, or group sessions in shared office spaces for co-located teams).
- **Key Framing**: Mental training operates on the same principles as physical training, with results directly correlated to consistent effort and input.
- **Session Preview**: This session covers "meditation in-action", a practice option designed to save time for busy participants.

### 2. Teaching: Mindfulness
- **Term Critique**: The word "mindfulness" has lost its specific meaning due to widespread popular hype, and is often used interchangeably with vague terms like "presence". Facilitators and participants are encouraged to adopt alternative, clearer terms (e.g., "wakefulness" or "being awake") to describe the targeted state.
- **Core Working Definition (Course-Specific)**: Mindfulness is defined as a state of full awareness of one's self (thoughts, feelings, breath) and surroundings, framed as *extreme single-tasking*: fully engaging with one activity at a time without distraction, as opposed to the common pattern of split attention (e.g., thinking about the next meeting during a current one, scrolling social media while watching a movie).
- **Course Scope**: This training focuses on the practical, day-to-day experience of single-tasking, rather than complex academic or spiritual definitions of mindfulness found in specialized texts.

### 3. Method: Ways to Practice Mindfulness
- **Core Practice**: Formal seated meditation with a focus on breath awareness is identified as the most effective method to build long-term mindfulness skill.
- **Informal Practice Options (Substitutes for Formal Meditation)**:
  - Mindful eating: Eating meals without phones or conversation, fully focusing on the taste and experience of food
  - Mindful walking: Slow, intentional walking with full awareness of each step and wandering thoughts
  - Mindful yoga
  - Mindful performance of routine activities (e.g., washing dishes)
- **In-Session Practice Options**:
  - If sufficient space is available: Lead a short mindful walking exercise
  - If space is limited: Lead a raisin eating meditation, or repeat prior breath-focused meditation (framed as "mindful breathing")
- **Weekly Homework Assignment**: Maintain 2 x 10-minute daily meditation sessions; participants may replace one or both sessions with an informal mindful activity (e.g., eating, walking) if desired.

### 4. Closing Reflection
- **Facilitation Guide**: Participants take turns counter-clockwise to reflect on how mindfulness could support stress management and improved focus.
- **Ground Rules**: Allow each participant to speak fully; answer all questions posed during the session, and record any unanswered questions for follow-up.
- **Session Close**: Inform participants the facilitator will stay after the session to address additional questions; thank participants for their time and attention.

## Extra Practice Material
1. Acceptance framing during meditation: Intentionally say "yes" to all experiences that arise during practice (restlessness, wandering thoughts, physical pain, memories, anxiety) and observe the outcome.
2. Consistency framing: Wandering attention is a normal, expected part of meditation. Follow the principle *"Fall seven times, get back up eight"*: return to your focus point each time your mind drifts, without judgment.
3. Body scan practice: A recommended opening for formal meditation sessions, involving sequential, focused awareness of each part of the body (feet, legs, stomach, etc.) to build bodily awareness.
