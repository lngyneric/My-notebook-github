# Team Awareness Training: Session #2 - Meditation

## Summary
This session is part of the Team Awareness Training curriculum, designed to teach participants core meditation fundamentals: its definition, practice methods, physiological and cognitive effects during practice, and non-spiritual philosophical framing for mental state development. The session includes group reflection on prior deep breathing practice, guided meditation teaching, hands-on practice, and post-practice reflection, with assigned daily homework to build consistent mental training habits.

## Session Agenda (Total ~60 Minutes)
| Session Component | Allotted Duration | Core Purpose |
|-------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Weekly Sharing & Reflection | 10 mins | Group check-in on prior week's deep breathing practice experience |
| Meditation Fundamentals Teaching | 20 mins | Core definition, variants, evidence-based effects, and program-specific framing of meditation |
| Guided Meditation Practice | 20 mins | Step-by-step instruction and supervised practice of basic breath-focused meditation |
| Post-Practice Group Reflection | 10 mins | Discussion of practice experience and links to the stress response covered in Session 1 |

## Facilitator Guidelines
- Document all key participant insights and questions during the session, and follow up on all items via post-session email.
- Do not rush participants during sharing or reflection rounds; avoid steering participant reflections unless explicitly asked to answer a question.
- Frame all mental skills practice as analogous to physical training: outcomes are directly correlated with consistent effort and input.
- Support participants who struggle to complete homework by problem-solving accessible practice timing and locations (e.g., home, work breaks, commutes, shared group sessions in a conference room for co-located teams).
- Do not shame participants for incomplete homework; instead, emphasize the value of consistent practice and coach them to build sustainable routines.
- Remain available after the formal session ends to address unresolved group questions and hold 1:1 conversations with participants.

## Core Session Content
### 1. Weekly Sharing
Facilitate clockwise turn-taking for participants to reflect on their prior week's deep breathing practice, covering:
- Perceived difficulty and comfort level of practice
- Ability to carve out time for regular practice
- Observed changes in response to stressful situations

### 2. Meditation Fundamentals Teaching
- **Core Definition**: Meditation is an umbrella term for techniques used to discover, develop, and express mental states. In its simplest form, it consists of maintaining awareness of one's inner and outer state.
- **Active Meditation**: A practice format where practitioners make a proactive effort to stay present: as soon as they notice attention drifting into daydreams, they release the distraction and return awareness to the current moment.
- **Practice Variants**: Meditation includes a wide range of formats, including focus on an image, a repeated word (mantra), movement (walking meditation, tai chi), or seated still practice.
- **Program-Specific Framing**: This curriculum uses meditation for mental state development, not spiritual or transcendental exploration, analogous to how singing is not inherently religious even if used in religious settings.
- **Evidence-Based Effects**: Recent research confirms meditation reduces stress and improves focus. Stress reduction occurs because uninterrupted still practice allows distracting thoughts and worries to dissipate. Note that physical exercise and sleep are more evidence-backed primary stress management tools, but meditation serves as a complementary or alternative option for those for whom those tools are insufficient or inaccessible.
- **Broader Purpose**: Meditation is primarily a tool for mental state discovery and development, not exclusively a stress management technique.

### 3. Guided Meditation Practice
#### Basic Practice Steps
1. Sit with a straight back
2. Close your eyes
3. Focus awareness on the sensation of breath moving in and out through the nose
4. Immediately return focus to the breath whenever attention drifts to daydreams or distractions

#### Facilitation Flow
1. First lead a 1-minute practice, then debrief participant experiences.
2. Reassure participants that racing thoughts and restlessness are normal, not signs of failed practice: instruct them to simply notice these sensations, including where restlessness presents in the body.
3. Lead a longer practice, with periodic ~1-minute reminders that restlessness and daydreaming are acceptable, and prompts to return attention to the breath.

#### Assigned Homework
Meditate for 10 minutes, twice per day. A successful practice session is defined as sitting for the full 10-minute duration and returning attention to the breath (or other focus anchor) at least once.

### 4. Post-Practice Reflection
- Facilitate turn-taking for participants to share their meditation experience, answer questions, and document unresolved questions for follow-up.
- Guide group discussion on the link between stress and fear, and how meditation may counteract the stress response covered in Session 1.
- Inform participants the facilitator will remain available post-session for additional questions and 1:1 conversations.

## Extra Practice Guidance
- The breath-focused meditation taught in this session may be referred to as mindfulness meditation or vipassana (insight) meditation.
- It can be helpful to open practice with a few rounds of deep breathing.
- Alternative focus methods (counting to 10, mantras, visualization) are acceptable; participants may choose the method that works best for them.
- Practice may be done seated in a standard chair; avoid leaning back on the chair support if feeling sleepy.
- Lying down during practice is not recommended for most people, as it typically leads to sleep.
